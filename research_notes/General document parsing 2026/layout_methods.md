# General-purpose document layout analysis

## What research says about layout variation and text + geometry signals

### Takeaway

Document layouts vary enough that models or rules tuned to one document family should not be treated as general-purpose. Text, 2D geometry, and page appearance are complementary signals: preserve all that is available, but keep the parser's basic representation model-agnostic and avoid making textual semantics or a single linear reading order the structure itself.

### Cited Findings

- DocLayNet's authors report that PubLayNet and DocBank were generated from scientific repositories and uniform publisher templates, and that models trained on them degrade on more diverse, free-style pages; their human-annotated DocLayNet spans 80,863 pages from six categories and has 11 visual/layout labels. Their cross-dataset comparison found the DocLayNet-trained detector more robust across datasets than detectors trained on the earlier scientific-paper datasets. [DocLayNet, KDD 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01062)
- DocLayNet's categories include financial reports, manuals, scientific articles, laws/regulations, patents and tenders, but its authors explicitly minimized scans and excluded receipts, invoices, handwriting and text-in-the-wild photos. Thus it is evidence for varied general page layouts, not direct evidence that its trained models cover all small business documents or scanned inputs. [DocLayNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.01062v1)
- DocLayNet's redundant annotation subset showed an inter-annotator mAP range of about 82–83 overall; baseline object detectors scored about 72–77 mAP, roughly 6–10 percentage points below the human agreement range. Annotation disagreements included reasonable alternatives such as grouping a multi-panel figure into one region or several, illustrating that even labels and segmentation boundaries can be ambiguous. [DocLayNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.01062v1)
- PubLayNet contains over 360,000 pages automatically aligned from PubMed Central XML and PDF content. Its authors demonstrated strong results on scientific articles and proposed transfer learning to other domains, while DocLayNet later documented the broader domain-coverage weakness of automated scientific-source annotations. [PubLayNet, ICDAR 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07836); [DocLayNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.01062)
- LayoutLMv3 fuses OCR text, normalized 2D word/segment boxes and image patches. Its pretraining includes masked language modeling, masked image modeling and word-patch alignment; its experiments cover text-centric tasks (forms, receipts, document QA) as well as image-centric layout analysis. It therefore supports the practical claim that spatial and visual cues add information beyond text alone, rather than prescribing a necessary architecture for a lightweight parser. [LayoutLMv3, ACM MM 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.08387)
- LayoutLMv3's layout-analysis experiment was evaluated on PubLayNet, which is scientific-paper imagery; its reported results do not imply universal performance on arbitrary layouts. The paper itself uses OCR text boxes and image inputs for its multimodal tasks. [LayoutLMv3](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.08387v3)
- Doc2Graph represents document objects as graph nodes and uses text, normalized absolute positions, visual features and relative geometric edge features. Its ablations on forms found geometry and text jointly useful; visual features provided a further gain at a substantial parameter cost. It also notes that heuristic graph connectivity may fail to generalize or exclude valid relations, although a fully connected learned graph has its own compute and training costs. [Doc2Graph, ICIAP 2022 / arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11168)
- LayoutReader used text and layout signals in a sequence-to-sequence order prediction model and introduced ReadingBank with 500,000 document images generated from Word documents. This shows the value of the pair of signals for reading-order prediction, but the training source's Word-origin distributions and permutation output limit the extent to which its findings settle order in every complex page. [LayoutReader, EMNLP 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.389/)
- Recent reading-order work argues a single permutation is an incomplete representation: complex pages may permit multiple valid sequences, parallel columns or independent header/footer/watermark flows. It proposes immediate succession as a directed acyclic relation and generalized succession as its transitive closure; in its ROOR sample, 23.76% of segments were involved in non-linear reading order. The paper reports downstream gains from injecting these relations, including across several cross-domain IE/QA settings. [Zhang et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.540/)
- PyMuPDF explicitly warns that PDF text extraction order may differ from natural reading order because content order depends on how the PDF was authored. Its `sort` option applies a top-left-to-bottom-right ordering heuristic; blocks and words include geometry, which can support more context-sensitive reconstruction. [PyMuPDF text extraction documentation](https://pymupdf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/recipes-text.html)
- A tagged PDF can provide a logical structure tree and content order; W3C accessibility guidance describes tag order as the relevant sequence for tagged documents. Treat this as a potentially useful source of authored structure, not a guarantee that all PDFs are tagged correctly. [W3C PDF3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/pdf/PDF3); [Well-Tagged PDF 1.0](https://pdfa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/Well-Tagged-PDF-WTPDF-1.0.pdf)

### Inferences

- Treat a document as a spatially grounded collection of elements plus typed relations, not as a string with coordinates attached. Preserve raw extraction order and geometry, and add a best-effort reading-order view that can be revised without destroying source order.
- Make visual layout analysis an optional capability/back-end. A parser that aims to stay lightweight can deliver robust extraction and geometry first; pretrained models should improve difficult region classification/order where users opt into their runtime and model dependencies.
- Evaluate domain generality by document-level splits and varied held-out categories, not random page splits. DocLayNet reports roughly a 10-point apparent gain from page-wise rather than document-wise splits, evidence that near-duplicate style leakage can overstate generalization. [DocLayNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.01062v1)
- Keep text labels such as “title”, “header”, “table” probabilistic or source-attributed. Visual role boundaries are sometimes genuinely ambiguous, and some roles (e.g. section heading) require context beyond an isolated rectangle.

### Gaps

- DocLayNet does not represent receipt/invoice layouts, handwriting or a broad scanned corpus; its domain coverage should not be cited as a benchmark for those cases. [DocLayNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.01062v1)
- No cited benchmark establishes one universally optimal region taxonomy or reading order for every language, directionality, document genre and mixed-content page.
- Results from LayoutLMv3, LayoutReader and graph methods are task- and dataset-specific. They motivate representation choices but do not prove a particular model will improve this repo's extraction quality without evaluation on its corpus.

## How a parser should represent blocks, relations, tables, figures, and key-value content

### Takeaway

Use a generic element graph/tree with stable IDs, normalized page geometry, source/provenance and confidence. Represent reading order and semantic relationships as explicit typed edges (allowing a DAG and alternatives), while tables retain a two-dimensional cell grid and figures/captions or key/value entities use links rather than category-specific hard-coded fields.

### Cited Findings

- DocLayNet chose visually identifiable, broadly recurrent labels—Caption, Footnote, Formula, List-item, Page-footer, Page-header, Picture, Section-header, Table, Text and Title—and explicitly avoided category-specific classes and roles tightly dependent on text semantics. Its annotation rules also require each caption to correspond to a figure or table. [DocLayNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.01062v1)
- DocLayNet describes plausible segmentation alternatives even under a shared label scheme; the annotation guidelines were important for consistency. Its authors note visual interpretation can differ from PDF typesetting structures (e.g. invisible layout tables used only for alignment are not necessarily semantic tables). [DocLayNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.01062v1)
- The 2024 reading-order formulation distinguishes local immediate-successor links (a DAG) from the transitive/global precedence relation (strict partial order), and explicitly discusses parallel flows, headers/footers/watermarks and horizontal/vertical table reading alternatives. [Zhang et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.540/)
- Doc2Graph uses nodes for words or grouped entities and edges for pairwise relationships; it models directed edges where relation direction matters. It discusses relative distance and angle as geometric edge features, as well as language, visual and absolute-position node features. [Doc2Graph](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.11168v1)
- PubTables-1M supplies table annotations for rows, columns and cells (including blank cells), headers, projected row headers, text and PDF/image coordinates. Its paper identifies oversegmentation and annotation inconsistency as a problem and introduces canonicalization; the official Table Transformer implementation treats table detection and structure recognition as separate tasks and needs independently extracted text to populate output cells. [PubTables-1M, CVPR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00061); [official Table Transformer repository](https://github.com/microsoft/table-transformer)
- Table structure is naturally two-dimensional. GriTS evaluates predicted tables in matrix form and unifies cell topology, location and content comparisons, supporting retaining grid structure rather than flattening a table prematurely into text or CSV. [GriTS, ICDAR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12555)
- Cloud OCR/layout APIs expose a useful production precedent: elements can have typed roles, polygons/spans, nested table-cell structures and generic relationships (e.g. AWS Textract's KEY and VALUE blocks have explicit relationship links). This is documentation of existing output models, not evidence that parser output should copy a vendor API. [Azure layout model](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/prebuilt/layout); [AWS AnalyzeDocument](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/APIReference/API_AnalyzeDocument.html)
- LayoutParser's research/toolkit contribution emphasizes shared data structures, model adapters, visualization and configurable pipelines for document image analysis. [LayoutParser, DAS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.15348)

### Inferences

- Suggested generic model:
  - `Document` → ordered `Page`s; each page records source dimensions, units, rotation/orientation and coordinate frame.
  - `Element` nodes have stable IDs, open-vocabulary `type` (e.g. `text_block`, `line`, `word`, `table`, `table_row`, `table_cell`, `figure`, `caption`, `header`, `footer`, `form_control`, `key`, `value`), normalized bbox or polygon, text, children, source (`pdf_text`, `ocr`, `layout_model`, `heuristic`), confidence and extensible attributes.
  - Relations are separately represented with `source_id`, `target_id`, relation `type`, confidence/provenance and optional direction. Useful generic types include `precedes` (DAG edge), `caption_of`, `contains`, `aligned_with`, `same_table`, `key_for`, `repeats_as_header`, and `continues_on_page`. Do not require all relation types from every backend.
  - Keep `reading_order` as a compatibility projection (deterministic topological linearization) while preserving the underlying predecessor/successor graph and ambiguous/nonlinear branches. Stable tie-breaking can use geometry, but do not pretend that the tie is authored fact.
  - A table is an element with a grid/rows/cells as children. Cells should preserve row/column indices, row/column spans, header role, bbox and text; empty cells matter. Preserve original OCR/native word nodes or references to them so table extraction does not duplicate or lose source evidence.
  - Figures should remain figure/image regions even when no text is available; captions are separate text elements linked to a figure/table, rather than appended in text and then lost as structure. Header/footer role should be optional and separable from the main content flow; repeated-position/text evidence across pages can mark likely repetition.
  - Key-value is a generic relation between visible key-like label and value-like content, not an invoice field ontology. Preserve the text and geometry of both nodes, permit one-to-many/many-to-one/uncertain links, and do not normalize into business-specific names at layout-analysis time.
- The repo already has a promising starting point: `src/fastdocparse/parsing/models.py` defines normalized `BBox`, recursive `ParsedElement` children, provenance (`source`), confidence and arbitrary attributes; `src/fastdocparse/parsing/pdf.py` extracts block→line→word and table→cell structures. Most directly useful extension is adding stable element IDs and explicit relations/order metadata while preserving current public output compatibility. [Repo: `models.py`](../../src/fastdocparse/parsing/models.py); [Repo: `pdf.py`](../../src/fastdocparse/parsing/pdf.py)
- Keep canonical layout parsing separate from task `Schema` (`src/fastdocparse/schema.py`): schemas should describe user-requested fields, whereas generic layout blocks and relationships are properties of the source document. This avoids hard-coding invoice semantics into reusable parser infrastructure. [Repo: `schema.py`](../../src/fastdocparse/schema.py)
- Keep output faithful: attach each reconstructed element to source word IDs where possible, expose whether a grouping was detector-derived or inferred, and avoid duplicate text when table regions supersede general text blocks. The PDF path already removes text overlapping detected tables; provenance and explicit membership can make this auditable. [Repo: `pdf.py`](../../src/fastdocparse/parsing/pdf.py)
- A nice minimum contract is geometry + hierarchy + evidence + relations, with semantic labels allowed to be `unknown`. Downstream consumers can choose flattened text, Markdown, a DAG, tables, or extraction schemas without forcing the parser to make irreversible assumptions.

### Gaps

- The cited reading-order relation paper studies text segments and does not define a complete schema for non-text figures, all table semantics, mixed RTL/LTR content, or cross-page links. Those recommendations above are architectural synthesis, not a directly standardized schema.
- There is no single cited, widely adopted common interchange standard that unifies OCR token geometry, visual regions, table grids, logical structure and relation confidence. DocLayNet itself observes the lack of an accepted format linking geometric and textual layout features. [DocLayNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.01062v1)
- Header/footer repeat detection, cross-page table continuation and key/value relation taxonomies remain document- and language-sensitive; confidence and alternatives should be preserved instead of claiming universal heuristics.

## Practical PDF stages and risks of heuristic-only grouping

### Takeaway

Route each page based on the quality and coverage of its text layer, not just whether any text exists. Native PDF extraction is fast and often precise but has no guaranteed reading sequence; scanned pages need rendering, orientation/deskew, OCR with boxes/confidence, and then the same downstream geometry/layout pipeline. Heuristics are valuable, cheap fallbacks, but cannot reliably infer arbitrary semantic grouping and should expose uncertainty rather than silently assert structure.

### Cited Findings

- PyMuPDF says PDF plain extraction returns text in the PDF's coded order and may have unusual order/line breaks; the `sort` option orders top-left to bottom-right but is only a heuristic. Native words and blocks carry positions, and spans expose formatting data (font, size, color, flags). [PyMuPDF text recipes](https://pymupdf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/recipes-text.html)
- PyMuPDF describes ordinary PDF tables as text laid out visually rather than embedded spreadsheet/table objects. Table extraction therefore must identify a region and infer cell borders using graphical lines or word alignment; `Page.find_tables()` is an available non-ML method but cannot replace a universal structure model. [PyMuPDF text recipes](https://pymupdf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/recipes-text.html)
- PyMuPDF documentation includes examples for combining table extraction and text, multiple tables on a page and table fragments across pages, underscoring these as explicit concerns in practical extraction. [PyMuPDF table-analysis examples](https://github.com/pymupdf/PyMuPDF-Utilities/tree/master/table-analysis)
- Tesseract's documentation says skew significantly reduces line segmentation quality and recommends rotating the page so text lines are horizontal. It offers different page segmentation modes, including automatic OSD and sparse-text modes; OCR configuration can therefore be selected based on page characteristics rather than assuming one mode fits every layout. [Tesseract quality guidance](https://tesseract-ocr.github.io/tessdoc/ImproveQuality.html)
- DocLayNet describes documents as programmatic, scanned or hybrid and notes that rotations/skews complicate annotation. Its dataset minimized scanned pages, so general layout detectors evaluated there are not proof of scan robustness. [DocLayNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.01062v1)
- The DocLayNet authors identify fixed-source/automatically generated data's variability constraint and report accuracy drops cross-domain; they identify ML misses/overlaps and inconsistent or ambiguous annotations as real limitations. [DocLayNet](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.01062v1)
- Doc2Graph cautions that heuristic graphs (e.g. visibility graphs) may generalize poorly, depend on earlier detection quality, create noisy links from imperfect boxes and preclude valid distant relations. Its own simple region proposal from connected table nodes is reported to be error-prone when a false positive is included. [Doc2Graph](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.11168v1)
- LayoutLMv3's visual-plus-text model found visual representations important for image-centric layout analysis; adding image patches without suitable visual pretraining degraded/diverged in an ablation, while image modeling/alignment objectives improved results. This cautions that simply adding rendered pixels to a text parser does not automatically create useful visual understanding. [LayoutLMv3](https://arxiv.org/html/2204.08387v3)
- A Google Document AI OCR API documents a hybrid native-PDF parsing mode that uses embedded digital text where available and optical OCR where not, returning a merged result. This supports a page-level/per-element hybrid route as an implementation pattern (vendor feature, not a universal algorithm). [Google Enterprise Document OCR](https://docs.cloud.google.com/document-ai/docs/enterprise-document-ocr)

### Inferences

- Suggested staged processing:
  1. Inspect PDF metadata, page dimensions/rotation, text spans/words, images, vector drawings and tags/structure if present.
  2. Score page-level native text quality and coverage: character count, replacement/garbage ratio, font/encoding validity, spatial coverage, visibility, image occupancy and coherence. Choose native extraction, OCR or hybrid on a page/region basis; do not switch solely on a low global character threshold.
  3. Native branch: extract positioned spans/characters and words; retain source order and formatting, group to lines and blocks using geometry, test for tables, and use optional tagged structure where valid. Record both content-stream and reconstructed order when they disagree.
  4. Image branch: render at configurable resolution; detect rotation/orientation, deskew, optionally clean up contrast/noise; run OCR to word/line level with geometry, text confidence and language; preserve the raster page/coordinates and transform OCR boxes back to the canonical page coordinate frame.
  5. Merge native text, OCR and visual blocks with overlap/deduplication that preserves provenance. A hybrid PDF may contain both native and scanned regions; do not treat duplicated OCR/native text as two separate content units.
  6. Infer layout regions/roles and candidate relations (reading order, table membership, caption associations, generic key-value) with separate confidence/provenance. Apply constrained rules and optional model backends; never overwrite raw evidence.
  7. Serialize structured elements and relations; offer a simple linearized text export as a derived view. Review via overlays and retain diagnostics such as page-level route, OCR confidence and inferred-vs-detected labels.
- For repository fit, `src/fastdocparse/parsing/pdf.py` currently implements native extraction and PyMuPDF table detection, while its own module documentation says image-only PDFs/scanned table reconstruction are not handled there. `src/fastdocparse/ocr/service.py` and `src/fastdocparse/ocr_engine.py` are current OCR integration points; separate page analysis/routing from field extraction to avoid coupling parsing to a task schema. [Repo: `pdf.py`](../../src/fastdocparse/parsing/pdf.py); [Repo OCR service](../../src/fastdocparse/ocr/service.py); [Repo OCR engine](../../src/fastdocparse/ocr_engine.py)
- The existing `_infer_aligned_tables` routine in `pdf.py` is explicitly described as a conservative unruled-table heuristic and not a replacement for learned structure recognition. A general parser should retain this as a low-cost candidate generator but make inference opt-in/auditable; avoid its lexical/numeric business assumptions becoming an implicit document taxonomy. [Repo: `pdf.py`](../../src/fastdocparse/parsing/pdf.py)
- Heuristic-only failure risks to test and surface:
  - Naive y/x sorting interleaves columns, sidebars, callouts, marginalia and inset captions; fixed tolerances vary with page size, font, rotation and scan skew.
  - Proximity/alignment can conflate forms, whitespace-aligned prose, side-by-side labels/values, invisible typesetting tables and true unruled tables. It can also miss irregular tables, nested/multiline cells, blank cells, spans and tables without repeated rows.
  - Numeric/string patterns overfit a genre or language (amounts, dates, identifiers) and risk mapping generic values to domain labels. Use geometry and repetition as evidence and keep role semantics unassigned unless separately supported.
  - Page edge heuristics for header/footer can misclassify top/bottom content and running headings; repetition across pages is useful evidence but can fail on chapter-specific headings, variable page sizes or one-page docs.
  - Caption adjacency is not always caption association; figures may have multiple subfigures/captions or no text. Use region/layout evidence and explicit uncertain links.
  - Scanned noise, orientation, skew, low resolution, handwriting, CJK/RTL scripts and OCR line segmentation errors propagate into all later spatial grouping. Keep OCR confidence, do not drop low-confidence content silently, and permit reprocessing with a better backend.
  - Arbitrary overlaps and multi-flow order can make a total sequence false precision. Store a partial order and the unresolved alternatives; linear order is merely one consumer-friendly rendering.
  - A mistaken early region/table detector can remove text from normal flow or duplicate it inside a table. Link source tokens to derived containers and keep an overlap/conflict audit trail.
- Calibrate conservative grouping on multiple document families, including scanned/mixed PDFs and pages with columns/forms/tables. Measure text coverage, relation precision/recall, table cell/grid correctness and reading-order pair accuracy; manually inspect failures. Do not tune or evaluate by randomly splitting pages from the same template/document.

### Gaps

- Sources establish the need for OCR preprocessing and hybrid extraction but do not establish one universal threshold for native text quality, render DPI, deskew method or page routing. Tune through a representative corpus and expose configuration.
- No heuristic can infer a single objectively correct reading flow for every visually rich page; the 2024 relation paper notes both multiple acceptable orders and ambiguity in table traversal. [Zhang et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.540/)
- A broad, directly comparable benchmark of lightweight rules versus learned layout models across invoices, statements, receipts, reports, forms, tables, figures and scanned pages was not identified. DocLayNet specifically excludes or minimizes several of those groups.
